07 / Evidence

Keep facts, interpretation, and decisions apart.

A useful technical notebook must let a future reader see not only what we concluded, but why the conclusion is credible and what could still be wrong.

Evidence hierarchy

  1. Primary source: original repository, source file, specification, or browser documentation.
  2. Observed result: a command output, published page, screenshot, or reproducible test, with the conditions recorded.
  3. Interpretation: what the evidence appears to mean, explicitly labelled as analysis.
  4. Decision: the action chosen from the evidence, with scope and status.

Rules for writing

  • Do not infer an author's intent from code alone when the commit or documentation does not say.
  • Do not call a visual difference intentional without an explicit decision.
  • Do not say “tested” if only source files were inspected.
  • Preserve source URLs, dates, revisions, and relevant file paths.
  • When evidence conflicts, state the conflict and choose what must be rechecked.

Reference and archive

The original project is XXIIVV/oscean. The repository's captured source material and earlier research are preserved under Archive. Those files are evidence and working history; the current decisions are summarised in the decision register.

When to revisit research

Recheck upstream when a question concerns a newer revision, the archive lacks the needed file, or the available evidence cannot settle a concrete issue. Do not repeat a research pass merely because the same source is easier to open than the local record.
